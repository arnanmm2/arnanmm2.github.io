Iran and Oman near deal to restore commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz

Iran and Oman are reportedly close to finalizing a framework agreement, reported 6-7 August, to restore commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz after months of disruption since the 2026 Iran war began in February. The framework would require vessels to pay maritime service fees through a newly created corporation rather than a full, unconditional reopening. President Trump said the broader Iran war could end "pretty soon," even as Abu Dhabi National Oil Co. reported continued attacks on transiting vessels. Roughly 13 million barrels a day — about a fifth of global oil consumption — normally moves through the strait.

InsightIndonesia imports a large share of its crude oil and LNG through Gulf routes feeding Hormuz, so any new fee regime or continued disruption keeps landed fuel costs elevated for Pertamina and the state subsidy budget.
Developing — Al Jazeera and CNBC both describe talks as "advancing positively," but no signed agreement was confirmed as of 7 August.

Deep dives

Why a weak jobs report just sent the stock market to record highs

TL;DR — Fewer new jobs usually signals cooling growth. But markets rallied because weak job growth makes a Fed hike less likely.
The mechanism

Growth-stock valuations lean on future profits discounted at the Fed's policy rate — when the Fed is expected to hold or cut, those future profits are worth more today. That's what happened 7 August: payrolls fell 23,000 vs +80,000 expected, and the Nasdaq jumped to a fresh high.

Why it matters to you

This "Fed on hold" dynamic is part of why the rupiah steadied and the IHSG rallied on the same trading day.

Go deeper: bls.gov/ces · federalreserve.gov.

The Strait of Hormuz: how one narrow channel can move oil prices everywhere

TL;DR — Roughly a fifth of the world's oil sails through Hormuz. Even talk of reopening it after months of disruption moves prices.
The mechanism

Hormuz is a "chokepoint" — no practical alternative route. In early August, Iran and Oman moved close to a framework restoring shipping, even as attacks continued — a mixed signal keeping Brent near $83-84.

Why it matters to you

Hormuz-driven oil-price risk flows almost directly into Pertamina's pump prices and the inflation figures shaping BI's rate decisions.

Go deeper: eia.gov "World Oil Transit Chokepoints".

From the Fed to your wallet: how US interest rates move the rupiah

TL;DR — When US rates are expected to stay high, capital flows toward dollar assets, weakening currencies like the rupiah.
The mechanism

Investors compare returns across countries — the "yield differential" effect. The Fed held at ~3.6% on 28-29 July; a weak July jobs report then cut hike odds, easing dollar strength broadly.

Why it matters to you

When the Fed backs off a hike, BI's job defending the rupiah gets easier.

Go deeper: bi.go.id · "interest rate parity" and "carry trade".

Reading an earthquake bulletin like a scientist: from "M7.7" to this week's "M6.4 near Karatung"

TL;DR — Magnitude is logarithmic — each whole step is ~32× more energy — and depth matters as much as size for tsunami risk.
The mechanism

This week's M6.4 near Karatung (5 August) is a case study: shallow (10km) but in the M6.1-6.9 damage-risk range, BMKG still ruled out a tsunami based on magnitude, depth and epicenter together.

Why it matters to you

Knowing how to read magnitude, depth and tsunami-potential labels lets you form your own first impression before checking BMKG's live feed.

Go deeper: inatews.bmkg.go.id · earthquake.usgs.gov.
